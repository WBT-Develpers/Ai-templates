A typical UK residential system runs from around £7,100 for a 3.68kWp array up to £9,695 for 7.36kWp, fully installed. Adding battery storage is roughly £1,200 more depending on capacity. Every Solar quote is fixed price with 0% VAT on domestic installations, and finance is available to spread the cost.

Yes, for most homes. A typical household saves around £1,260 a year once solar and battery are combined with the Smart Export Guarantee, which puts payback in the region of six to eight years on a system designed to last 25 to 30. Your surveyor will model your actual figures from your bills rather than a national average.

They do. Modern panels generate from diffuse daylight, not just direct sun, so an overcast day still produces, just less than a bright one. A home battery smooths this out by storing surplus from sunnier periods for use whenever you need it, day or night.

Most domestic systems are fitted, commissioned and switched on within a single day. Larger arrays, battery retrofits or jobs needing a consumer unit upgrade may run to two. Scaffolding usually goes up the day before and comes down a day or two after, we agree the whole schedule with you in advance.

A standard solar system shuts down in an outage for the safety of engineers working on the network. If keeping power on matters to you, we can specify a battery with whole home backup, such as the Tesla Powerwall 3, which islands your home from the grid and keeps essential circuits running.

For the vast majority of UK homes, solar falls under permitted development and needs no planning application. Listed buildings and conservation areas are the exception, and we'll tell you at survey stage if yours is affected. We handle the DNO notification and MCS registration either way.

Very little. British rain keeps panels reasonably clean, and there are no moving parts. We recommend a performance check every few years and a clean if output drifts, and our care plans include remote monitoring that flags a fault before you'd spot it on your bill.

It comes down to your evening consumption, not your roof. We look at your half hourly usage and size the battery to cover the load between sunset and bedtime, usually 5.8kWh for a smaller home, 8.7kWh for a family, and 14.5kWh where there's an EV or heat pump. Batteries are stackable, so you can add capacity later.
